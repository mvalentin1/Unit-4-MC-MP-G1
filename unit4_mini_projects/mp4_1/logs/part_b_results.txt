Even n=4 (dense clutter) leaves 20+ dB on NB-IoT/LTE-M/LoRaWAN, so the RANKING of the three is insensitive to n;
only RedCap is sensitive: its link closes at all only for n < 3.05 (and never with the 10 dB design margin). Battery life does not depend on n in this model, so the
choice is driven by infrastructure/fees, not by propagation uncertainty.

Task 4 - fleet-tracked shipping container (ocean crossing)
  nearest tower    10 km: PL =  126.5 dB vs MCLs NB-IoT 152 (ok), LTE-M (CE-B) 148 (ok), LoRaWAN SF12 151 (ok), 5G RedCap 119 (FAIL)
  nearest tower   100 km: PL =  161.5 dB vs MCLs NB-IoT 152 (FAIL), LTE-M (CE-B) 148 (FAIL), LoRaWAN SF12 151 (FAIL), 5G RedCap 119 (FAIL)
  nearest tower  1000 km: PL =  196.5 dB vs MCLs NB-IoT 152 (FAIL), LTE-M (CE-B) 148 (FAIL), LoRaWAN SF12 151 (FAIL), 5G RedCap 119 (FAIL)
  A single-distance budget cannot justify a choice here: (1) the question is not 'is the margin positive at one
  distance' but 'is any terrestrial tower within reach at all' - mid-ocean the nearest one is hundreds of km away and
  beyond the radio horizon, so every terrestrial option fails regardless of MCL; (2) the distance, the serving carrier and
  the roaming agreement change continuously along the route (truck, rail, port, ship); (3) mobility/handover and roaming
  are binary capabilities that a dB figure cannot express; (4) the ocean leg needs a different link budget altogether
  (satellite/NTN: ~500-2,000 km LEO slant range, Doppler, long delay). Hence the unit's answer: LTE-M/RedCap inland + NTN at sea.

RECOMMENDATION: LoRaWAN SF12 with 1-2 owned gateways. Margin 27.9 dB at 8 km, est. life 27 yr (real life is capped by cell shelf life ~10-15 yr), no recurring fees. NB-IoT/LTE-M close the link on paper but need a carrier network that is not there and would add per-device fees across 200 units; LTE-M is also the worst on battery (4.0 yr, below the 5-yr target).
